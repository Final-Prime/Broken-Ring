# History and Cosmology — adventure-runtime additions

This file only resolves facts that `gameplay-lab`'s `canon/HISTORY_AND_COSMOLOGY.md`
explicitly leaves open. It does not restate or override that source. Only the
facts below are new, decided here for this project's own canon going forward.

## The Cataclysm

Source status in `gameplay-lab`: cause, whether it created the Broken Ring, and
whether it severed the heavens are all `OPEN`.

Resolved here: the Cataclysm was a colossal meteor impact, millions of years
before any of the events below. The impact crater's rim is the Broken Ring's
mountain wall — the isolation the source describes is literal: for ages,
nothing could cross the crater wall in or out. Over the following millions of
years the crater floor recovered its own ecosystem, seeded only by whatever
rare life filtered in over millennia. The impactor fell from somewhere outside
the world entirely — deep space, or perhaps another universe; its true nature
remains unknown even now — and is also the source of Trythium itself, which
explains why the material's strangeness and its interaction with magic trace
to no known process native to this world. A strike of this magnitude, arriving
from wherever the divine planes don't naturally reach, is as good a candidate
explanation as any for the "silent heavens," alongside the doctrinal
possibilities `gameplay-lab` already leaves as `SUPERPOSITION`.

The impact itself came in low and from the east rather than straight down —
an oblique strike, which is why the crater's rim rises highest in the west
and never fully closed on the eastern side at all. See this repo's
`GEOGRAPHY.md` for what that shape means for the realm today.

Left open here too: what later broke the crater wall specifically in the
south, letting the Great Desert in.

## The Great Migration

Source status in `gameplay-lab`: who migrated, from where, why, and whether it
affected every people are all `OPEN`.

Resolved here: long after the Cataclysm, a second wave of falling entities —
the **DarkFall** — struck the wider world outside the Ring: void entities that
fell like smaller meteors and drained the life from everything they touched.
Some could reshape themselves mid-fall to steer their own trajectory toward a
chosen landing site rather than simply falling where gravity took them. The
land beyond the crater is believed, at least in theory and never confirmed by
any surviving account, to have been left empty and dead. Over eons, survivors
crossed by boat to reach the Ring's eastern shore; those who couldn't make the
crossing didn't survive. This is the migration bards' fragmented pre-Migration
histories refer to.

## Trythium's protection

Not addressed by `gameplay-lab`'s mechanical Trythium entries (which describe
what it does, not where it came from or why the Ring alone kept its life).
Resolved here: Trythium is why the Ring specifically survived the DarkFall
when the outside world apparently didn't — the dampening effect on arcane and
divine energy already established in `RING_TRAITS.md`/`MAGIC.md` extends to
whatever the void entities' draining influence actually is.

## The blood-trees and the Assembly Glade

`gameplay-lab` says only that the Assembly Glade is "a sacred natural clearing
formed by a circle of immense trees" deep in Lorienvale, without explaining why
that circle exists or why Lorienvale's forest and crimsonwood are red at all.

Resolved here: directly beneath the Cataclysm impact — the way a shockwave
falling from directly overhead, rather than arriving from the side, left the
trees under it standing at Tunguska in 1908 — a ring of ancient trees kept
their trunks while every branch was stripped away. In the same instant, raw
Trythium falling with the impactor infused them, and instead of dying they
were locked into a permanent, unresolved state between life and death: not
healthy, not decaying, simply arrested. That ring is the Assembly Glade's
circle of trees — the oldest, most literal blood-trees in Lorienvale, and the
reason the Assembly meets nowhere else. The wider forest's red color and
crimsonwood's odd resistance to fire and magic (`gameplay-lab`'s "Trythium-
laced waters... stain or infuse the forest") is the same infusion, diluted and
carried outward by groundwater over the millions of years since — a separate,
later contributor to the same crimson forest is the Solari lineage described
in this repo's `PEOPLES.md`.
