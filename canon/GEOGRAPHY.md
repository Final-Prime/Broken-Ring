# Geography — adventure-runtime additions

This file only resolves facts that `gameplay-lab`'s `canon/GEOGRAPHY.md`
explicitly leaves open — principally its "Scale locks" section, which says
most settlements, roads, and distances "remain OPEN until deliberately
designed." It does not restate or override that source's `LOCKED`/
`SOURCE_CANON` facts (the realm's overall shape, its five named powers, etc.).

## Scale

Resolved here: the Ring is roughly 380 miles east-west and 275 miles
north-south at its widest — closer to a large country than a true
continent, consistent with `gameplay-lab`'s own "not a small enclosed
country" while still letting several kingdoms fit without crowding. On this
repo's hex map (54×39 columns/rows), that works out to roughly 7 miles per
hex. `gameplay-lab`'s own "regional travel can take days or weeks" checks
out at normal foot/wagon pace across distances this size.

## Why there is no eastern wall

Extends this repo's `HISTORY_AND_COSMOLOGY.md#the-cataclysm`. The impactor
came in low and from the east, not straight down. Oblique impacts of this
kind produce an asymmetric crater: a high, complete rim on the far
(western) side, and a low, broken one on the near (eastern) side, where the
initial blast threw material outward rather than piling up a wall. This is
the literal, physical reason `gameplay-lab`'s own geography already
describes no eastern mountain wall and "no far shore... verified" — there
never fully was one to find. What little rose there has since eroded and
now sits mostly submerged: a field of jagged, broken footings just under
the waves. This is also a plausible, mundane explanation (not necessarily
the only one) for `gameplay-lab`'s own `RUMOR` that ships travelling too
far into the Vestrielle Sea do not return — they founder on the drowned
remnant of the eastern rim.

The crater floor itself was never a basin or a lake, for the same reason:
with no complete eastern wall, water draining toward the sea always had
somewhere to go rather than pooling. The interior stayed relatively flat
instead, filled in over millions of years by sediment and impact debris —
this is the ground Elenvale farms today.

## The central peak

Large, complex craters characteristically rebound at the point of impact
into a raised central peak — the same mechanism that produces the
momentary jet after a dropped object strikes a liquid surface, just acting
on rock for a few catastrophic seconds instead of water. Resolved here: the
Ring has exactly this feature, a raised highland roughly at the map's
geographic center.

## Climate is local, not latitudinal

None of the Ring's climate variety needs a continental distance to make
sense, because none of it is actually driven by simple latitude:

- The north is cold because it is mountainous — elevation, not distance
  from a pole. The gradient can compress into a short horizontal span the
  same way a real-world peak carries snow far closer to the equator than
  its lowland surroundings would suggest.
- The southern desert's spread is not natural aridity — `gameplay-lab`
  itself already ties the southern blight to the Void, a corrupting
  influence with no reason to respect ordinary climate-zone distances.
- Lorienvale's perpetual-twilight canopy is a Trythium-driven distortion,
  not a natural forest biome either.

A compact Ring is therefore not a contradiction. Each zone has its own
local cause, and none of them needed thousands of miles to make sense.
