PRELUDE · BEFORE BLOOD & TIMBER

Sand & Silver

The first bite of the feast

Begin reading ↓

Not the strongest arm in his year. Not the sharpest blade. Not even a name worth remembering. But he was alive when it ended, and that alone couldn’t be said of everyone. Garret was one of them.

Maybe that was luck. Maybe it was simpler: the Crown of Elenvale had no war to send him to, and when a year of training ran its course, young soldiers went home instead of to a battlefield. He was one of them, too.

Being alive suited him

Half-dreaming, Garret walked the wide path in the tired light of a sun making ready for bed. Its rays, dyed red and still sleepless, spread across the slow-changing land and struck up a dance with the fireflies, to a tune the swaying grass kept humming. Garret loved the quiet. Loved the stillness. And still, beautiful as the land was, in secret he could hardly wait to reach home, the southern farmstead he hadn’t laid eyes on in far too long.

What would they be doing right about now? Maybe bringing in the harvest. Maybe the fields already stood bare, and they’d taken to the porch, coffee in hand, the bulk of the work safely behind them. They weren’t far now...

Even the sun is heading home

And the stories he had for them. What would they say when they heard how five of them, barely a step out of the academy, had run down a Shroud mage on the Crown’s own orders? he thought, crossing a tidy stone bridge that reared over a brook. Would they believe him? Or just reckon he was stretching it? Well... either way, the girls wouldn’t be turning away from a story like that. Not even Sylma. It wasn’t the kind of thing you saw just anywhere. He smiled: the Crown’s hero, come home.

At the thought he stood a little straighter and, hardly knowing he did, quickened his stride. Oh, they’d see who’d come home. They’d see.

The stories weigh nothing yet

He knew he had reached the fort only because the bright moon went out behind the massive watchtower. That was how much night had come on while he walked. As he came around past the moat the torchlight struck him in the eyes, friendly and flickering and warm. A guard stood there, or at any rate was propped upright on his spear, snuffling softly.

Then the spear came down across the road, and the man behind it was very much awake.

“Who goes there at this hour?”

“A soldier.” Garret raised a hand. “Heading home.”

He waited for the spear to come up. It stayed where it was.

The gate has learned new manners

“If that’s what you are, let’s see your ghost-leaf.”

Garret handed it over, and there was more pride in the gesture than the moment called for. The guard turned the silver in the torchlight, found whatever he was looking for, and passed it back. The spear went up.

“Since when does Forest’s End keep a watch after dark?” Garret said. “Not even in the last war. I don’t recall a living soul standing at this gate, not for a fort this size.”

The guard’s eyes came up off the road. “You know a deal about this gate, for a man who only said he was passing through it.”

“I’m from here.” Garret heard himself explaining and could not stop. “Well. Was. Two years gone, near enough. I’m coming off training, that’s all.” And then, reaching for easier ground: “Harvest in yet?”

Something in the guard came out of its brace. He looked at Garret properly for the first time, and what was in his face was not suspicion anymore.

“Things have changed here.” He shifted his weight. “They weren’t good when you left either, but since then... since then there’s been...” He cut it off. “It isn’t wise to be on the road after dark. Not alone. And not south.”

A hero explains himself

“Changed how?”

“You know how it goes.” The guard hedged. “People tell this and that, all sorts. That the desert came alive and carried off their houses. That nothing grows in the south anymore. That the dead get up and walk among the living, that the sand gives birth to them. That the queen dances naked in the tavern next door.” He shrugged. “Each one harder to swallow than the last. And every one of them gets louder if you stand the man a round.”

Garret laughed. “Traders always talk.” He hitched his pack higher and started through the gate. “Two days, and I’m home.”

The guard watched him go. Or watched the little silver leaf go, catching the torchlight as it went. He had not really looked at it the first time.

“They don’t hand those out to the brave,” he said, to nobody in particular. “They hand them to whoever’s left.”

Warnings keep no one who won’t be kept

Garret Stone had counted the miles. Fifty. Twenty. Five. Nothing prepared him for the fifty-first, where there was nothing at all, only red-gold sand as far as the eye reached.

The counting stops before the walking does

Garret (barely a whisper) “...Tam?”

The Hollow doesn’t answer. But for one second its head tilts, exactly the way Tam Ostler’s always did, every time someone shouted his name across a yard.

Some things the sand didn’t take

No one knows how long he stayed there. Only that when he stood again, he didn’t walk south.

He didn’t walk south
